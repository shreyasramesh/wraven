Miscut Time

A dial printed off-register, like a miscut trading card, that still tells perfect time.

Reads
From the main left dial. A second, shorter hand set on the right completes the scale.
Dial & case
Soft matte guilloche dial split into a large left field (12–6) and a right crescent (10, 9, 8). Silver baton hands, red second hand, black crocodile-pattern strap.
The trick
As a hand tucks under the thickened bezel on the left, a matching duplicate hand emerges on the right. Two hand sets, one portal handoff.

The Equation Watch

Possibly the hardest watch in the world to read. Every glance is a maths problem.

Reads
Solve the equation on the dial: (A × B × C + D) × E + F. Six digit windows can build any time from 1:00 to 12:59.
Dial & case
Clean guilloche dial with cut-out digit windows, printed equation symbols, a flip AM/PM indicator and small seconds. Shown in purple, red and a translucent ghost version.
The trick
Two nested disk-and-ring assemblies rotate independently to change the digits.

Time Tricks

Perpetual Traffic

A traffic jam as a seconds display. The cars creep forward. The gap travels backwards.

Reads
Hours and minutes from hands in the dial. Seconds from the cars and the gap moving around a three-lane highway ring at the edge.
Dial & case
Road-sign indices, abstract cars on a perimeter track, shown in green and red versions with matching straps and a silver case.
The trick
A hidden hand under the dial carries a magnet. Magnetised cars on one-way rails get repelled forward into the gap ahead, so the gap moves backwards through the jam, forever. A magnetic barrier would be needed near a mechanical movement.

A separate Short (jFdJ4oJaaPE) shows this watch in traffic-light colourways.
